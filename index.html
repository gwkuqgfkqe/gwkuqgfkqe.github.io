Biomedical Engineering · Brown University

Zonghan Du

I study how brain–computer interfaces can recognize intended movements as neural recordings change over time.

I’m a master’s student at Brown. At BrainGate, I work with John Simeral on decoding hand gestures across sessions. At Massachusetts General Hospital, I work with Todd Herrington on how neural activity relates to approach–avoidance decisions.

Research

BrainGate · Brown University

Decoding gestures across days

Can a decoder recognize the same attempted movement as neural recordings change from one session to the next?

BrainGate · Brown University

Aligning finger movements 13 months apart

We used flow matching to align motor cortical recordings from two sessions covering the same 31 individuated finger gestures.

Massachusetts General Hospital

Neural activity before approach–avoidance decisions

In 30 participants, I tested when intracranial recordings could predict an upcoming approach–avoidance choice.

Fudan University

Decoding movement through dynamics

I studied whether a neural decoder could predict a small set of movement controls and use them to generate smooth trajectories.
