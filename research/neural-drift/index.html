Neural population structure

Modeling neural drift

Optimal transport for longitudinal intracortical BCIs.

BrainGate · Brown UniversityOngoing research

Do longitudinal neural representations follow structured trajectories that can support stable decoding?

Why this question matters

A decoder trained in one recording session may deteriorate when the neural population representation changes. Explicitly modeling these changes could help disentangle representational drift from loss of task information.

My contributions

  • Investigating cross-session changes in gesture-related intracortical population representations.
  • Exploring optimal transport and flow matching as mappings into a reference decoding space.
  • Using PCA–CCA alignment and a fixed decoder as a linear comparison.
  • Examining preservation of gesture-related latent geometry alongside decoding performance.

Current progress

The project examines gesture-related latent geometry and whether transport models can characterize longitudinal changes and reduce recalibration.

Next questions

Test whether drift follows predictable transport trajectories and whether this can reduce recalibration. These are open questions.

Scope of the evidence

Structured transport trajectories and reduced recalibration are research goals, not established outcomes.
