Neural activity before approach–avoidance decisions

In 30 participants, I tested when intracranial recordings could predict an upcoming approach–avoidance choice.

Analysis

In Todd Herrington’s group, I built a within-participant pipeline for stereo-EEG (sEEG) spectral power. I compared time-averaged, time-resolved, and temporal-generalization analyses. Linear classifiers, tree ensembles, CNNs, and GRUs were evaluated on matched trial-level splits.

Timing and validation

Activity in the second before the response predicted the upcoming choice above chance (cluster-corrected p = .0099). Feature selection and preprocessing used training data only. I also examined regional and frequency contributions using ablation, held-out permutation importance, and participant-level analyses.
