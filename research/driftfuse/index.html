DriftFuse

Preserving task structure across changing neural recordings.

BrainGate · Brown UniversityOngoing research

How can a decoder preserve gesture separation when session drift overlaps task-relevant neural variation?

Why this question matters

Long-term intracortical interfaces need to retain useful gesture information even as recordings change. The goal is to preserve class structure without assuming that all session information must disappear.

My contributions

  • Built independent clean and recording-variability-augmented encoders with supervised contrastive learning, fixed fusion and cosine-prototype decoding.
  • Designed a leakage-audited retrospective evaluation across five within- and cross-session protocols.
  • Used matched ablations, perturbations and geometric analyses to examine branch complementarity and task separation.

Results & evaluation

The retrospective evaluation spans seven datasets from two participants, five within- and cross-session protocols, and five optimization seeds. Mean trial macro-F1 was 68.88% across 35 dataset–protocol settings, 3.08 percentage points above the strongest complete-system baseline (Transformer).

Scope of the evidence

These results come from a retrospective offline evaluation; prospective closed-loop performance has not been established.
