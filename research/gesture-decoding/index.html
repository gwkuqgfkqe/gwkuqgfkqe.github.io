BrainGate · Brown University

Decoding gestures across days

Can a decoder recognize the same attempted movement as neural recordings change from one session to the next?

Approach

I developed CoDCR, a supervised contrastive decoder that brings recordings of the same gesture closer together across sessions while separating different gestures. I evaluated it on intracortical recordings from one BrainGate participant with tetraplegia across three tasks with 10–48 gesture classes.

Across-session performance

CoDCR achieved the highest mean trial-level macro-F1 on all three tasks, both with and without labeled calibration. Overall macro-F1 was 78.78%, 8.19 percentage points above NoMAD, the strongest baseline. Compared with raw features, CEBRA, and a GRU, the learned representations showed lower relative centroid drift and tighter within-class grouping relative to the distance between classes.

Calibration

On a ten-class finger task, pretraining followed by calibration with 150 labeled trials (about five minutes) achieved 85.77% macro-F1. Training from scratch with 530 labeled trials achieved 80.20%. These comparisons describe offline decoding for this participant.
